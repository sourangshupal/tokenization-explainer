A 62-year-old with ICD-10-CM E11.65 and chronic kidney disease stage 4 presents with community-acquired pneumonia.
Chest film shows left lower lobe consolidation. Start empiric therapy. Document β-lactam allergy.
Home medicines: empagliflozin 10 mg daily, metformin 1000 mg twice daily, levothyroxine 75 μg daily.
Labs: serum creatinine 1.4 mg/dL, hemoglobin A1c 8.2%, NT-proBNP 4200 pg/mL, troponin I elevated.
Oncology history: HER2-negative breast cancer and a BRCA1 pathogenic variant on germline testing.
Neurology prescribed an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor last month for memory loss.
Immunology: Naïve CD4+ T cells were measured; COVID-19 mRNA vaccine records are complete.
If influenza is confirmed, oseltamivir 75 mg twice daily for five days.
Pharmacy: vancomycin trough 18 μg/mL; adjust for serum creatinine 1.4 mg/dL.
Cardiology ruled in ST-elevation myocardial infarction overnight; cath lab activated.
Do not confuse ICD-10-CM E11.65 with other diabetes codes in the billing worksheet.
Empagliflozin 10 mg daily was held on the morning of contrast CT, then restarted.
The acetylcholinesterase inhibitor can cause nausea; take with food.
BRCA1 pathogenic variant carriers need intensified breast and ovarian surveillance.
Left lower lobe consolidation improved after treatment of community-acquired pneumonia.
Units matter: mg/dL for serum creatinine 1.4 mg/dL, μg for levothyroxine 75 μg daily, pg/mL for NT-proBNP 4200 pg/mL.
β-lactam allergy is not the same as an intolerance; clarify the reaction in the chart.
COVID-19 mRNA vaccine myocarditis is rare; chest pain still needs troponin I elevated workup.
